## Acceptance checklist
1. Stable lesson and chapter IDs with independently authored VI/EN titles (unless an intentionally shared proper name is justified), clear goals and entry/resume. Resolve titles by ID and active locale in Reader, Catalog, Search (both title aliases searchable), breadcrumb, related lessons, bookmarks, resume/progress and curriculum filters. Never assign the same English title to VI and EN as a fallback for an authored lesson.
2. Lesson-linked Home Gương hook (intriguing question + context; direct route).
3. Separate fictional or sourced Problem Case → Khảo Chứng → Phản Chứng; evidence label, choices, branch, feedback, reasoning prompt, bookmark/saved case; never reuse an unrelated case silently.
4. Intro, concept explanation, examples, meaningful first-party or licensed visual (accessible text, alt/fallback; no decorative quota).
5. Guided worked example + a distinct learner application question + matching self-check after meaningful saved response. An already-solved example alone is not a practice task. Preserve old drafts without treating them as a new graded attempt.
6. Independent case, four questions under current reader contract, editable draft, optional staged hint that does not reveal final answers; record assistance truthfully.
7. Relevant primary technique or optional method recommendation with rationale; actual in-context practice and gated feedback when applicable; link to existing Sổ Tay. Do not force all 13 into every lesson. Record task alignment in the existing lesson ontology projection: canonical primary/optional IDs, actual practice and independent-task source references, bilingual observable actions, and evidence limits. Review real learner actions against technique definitions rather than matching IDs or method names. Optional notes are not separate enacted attempts. Task alignment does not establish measured effectiveness, mastery, or academic/publication approval.
8. Six context-specific Elder Ly moments (opening, concept, guided, independent, reflection, extension), VI/EN, safe staged actions/quote rights. No generic substitute for lesson context.
9. Copy & open ChatGPT at guided, independent, feedback and progress; clipboard only, user explicitly pastes/sends; no hidden answer before reveal and no private content in URLs.
10. Valid submission unlocks model answer and four rubric rows; explicit self-review, no invented scores/mastery.
11. Real progress/resume, restart confirmation, due-review/T02, no duplicate locale or method completion.
12. A dedicated Sources & further reading (Đào sâu) section in the reading phase, matching the shared LessonSources component used by the three Wave1 lessons. Include relevant, working HTTPS references with title, purpose, and checked scope/limitations; distinguish core and further reading as appropriate. Provide Open source, individual source Copy Prompt, combined Copy Prompt, Open ChatGPT, and lesson-specific Suggested searches wired to the existing in-app search. Localize section labels and source explanations (VI/EN); search queries may be English technical terms where useful. Inline citations alone, a single verification prompt, or inert keyword chips do not satisfy this requirement. Use sufficient sources and queries for the actual lesson, not arbitrary quotas. Preserve related accessible lesson(s) with rationale and record material academic uncertainty; rights/release metadata remains separate from personal-study completeness.
13. Ontology: show lesson-level concept IDs, bilingual labels, typed meaningful relation(s), explanation, evidence/review status and connection to canonical graph IDs when available. Reuse identities; proposed concepts/edges stay editorial and never enter verified search/graph or become prerequisites automatically. Keep link to actual lesson source. No fabricated edges or fixed concept quota.
14. Analogy: show a specific everyday comparison with correspondence to the actual concept and explicit limits. Never use analogy as evidence or reveal independent answers. Both locales; preserve rigorous explanation.
15. Bilingual acceptance requires actual rendered VI and EN views, not just fields or static assertions. Verify all five learning phases and actual localized headings, questions, source/search actions, mentor and rubric labels, feedback and progress; switch VI→EN→VI while preserving the same lesson ID, stage, saved draft, submission, assistance and review state. Check title consistency on discovery and private shelves. Use the existing isolated browser QA with synthetic receipts and review material translation quality; record any missing content/translation or blocked live/device evidence explicitly. Do not auto-translate source titles, technical proper names or learner-authored text. A source-shape/compile PASS is not bilingual experience acceptance. Verify affected controls/gates and academic claims; avoid unrelated regression.
